[12] hv_reference_robustness_1016.mat
     Offline robustness audit of the terrain statistics. Nothing is re-optimised:
     the archived 30-run fronts of both scenarios are re-scored under four
     reference boxes (pooled-baseline, pooled-all, x1.10, x0.90) and K = 20
     independent Monte-Carlo draws of the HV estimator, and all six A/B/C/D
     pairwise contrasts are reported (IGD as a deterministic, box-independent
     control). ~9 min, single thread.
     -> Section 3.4 (the scenario asymmetry, the box sensitivity, the estimator
        noise), Table R1-a of the response letter.
     Human-readable output: logs/hv_reference_robustness_1016.txt

[13] g_schedule_ablation_1016.mat
     Ablation of the amplitude envelope G(t) alone. Three arms of the same
     algorithm (all = full MO-ALA, variant D) that differ only in the envelope:
       linear = 2(1 - t/(Tmax-1))   [the rule used in the manuscript]
       const  = 2                   [no damping]
       floor  = 2(1 - 0.9 t/(Tmax-1))  [decays to 0.2, never 0]
     Six benchmarks, T = 500, 30 runs, true common random numbers across arms
     (all three arms use REF_AI = 4, so the `linear` arm is seed-identical to the
     archived MOALA-D(original) column of ablation_v8_degradation_1016.mat; the
     script re-checks this element-wise). 21 min 17 s wall on five workers.
     Result: the envelope is functional but not a universal optimum -- reducing
     the damping (floor) degrades the deterministic IGD significantly on ZDT1
     (+4.22%, p = 0.0013), ZDT3 (+4.18%, p = 0.0018) and DTLZ2 (+4.84%, p = 0.014),
     and removing it (const) does so on DTLZ2 (+3.89%, p = 0.045), while on ZDT1
     the undamped arm is significantly better on both metrics (-5.40% IGD,
     p = 3.1e-05; +0.18% HV, p = 0.0023). Hypervolume is identically 0 for all
     three arms on DTLZ1 and DTLZ3, an independent reproduction of the
     reference-anchoring floor in 3.1.
     -> reviewer 4's "artificial damping" mechanism objection, and reviewer 1's
        question about convergence stability; cited in the response letter under
        R1-2 (full result, including the ZDT1 counter-example) and R4-2, and it
        motivates the new Section 5.2 future-work paragraph on a convergence-
        adaptive envelope. Every number above is printed, unedited, by the log.
     Human-readable output: logs/g_schedule_ablation_1016.txt

C. NOTES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* The scripts dated 2026-09-15 (legacy) retain their original comments, some of
  which are in Chinese; they are shipped unmodified so that the archived results
  remain byte-for-byte reproducible from the original submission.
* Every script resolves its inputs and outputs from its own location, so none
  depends on the current working directory and none has to be edited. The four
  experiment scripts that call the withheld core implementation list the required
  files in their header and stop with an explicit ":WithheldCode" error before
  doing any work, rather than failing later with "Undefined function".
* The tolerance/threshold constants used by the audit are theta_max = pi/3,
  h_min = 2 m and a [0, 100] x [0, 100] flight box, as in Table 2 of the paper.
* audit_hv_reference_robustness_1016.m reads the archived baseline fronts from
  data/terrain_metrics.mat (the published terrain main-experiment archive that
  backs Tables 11-13). Its allFronts cells are bit-identical to the
  results/expResult.mat used when the audit was first produced, verified
  front-by-front over all 2 scenarios x 6 algorithms x 30 runs = 360 cells, so
  the shipped archive reproduces the published audit exactly.
